PSY-01 Claremont McKenna College · PSYC110 · 2025

Dearly Beloved, You've Hurt Me

Design
2×3×2×2 factorial survey, 194 data points (97 participants)
Role
Designed study, ran analysis, wrote paper
Outcome
Best-friend relationships proved resilient; romantic ones gender-sensitive

Hypothesis. Harm severity, relationship type, and gender jointly shape whether people forgive an offense and stay in the relationship.

Method. Participants read a randomized low- and high-harm scenario involving a best friend, boyfriend, or girlfriend, then rated relationship damage, forgiveness, and intent to end things.

Result. Harm level drove nearly every outcome (p < .001), but its effect depended entirely on context — best friends were forgiven equally regardless of harm, while romantic relationships showed sharp, gender-specific sensitivity to offense severity.

PSY-02 Claremont McKenna College · Conference Poster · 2025

Impact of Physical Attractiveness on Helping Behavior

Design
60 staged field trials, 4 counterbalanced appearance conditions
Role
Co-designed study, coded observations, co-authored poster
Outcome
Attractiveness lengthened help given, but didn't change who got helped

Hypothesis. More attractive confederates would receive more frequent and effortful help from bystanders, especially from opposite-gender helpers.

Method. A confederate staged an "accidental spill" in public settings while presenting one of four appearance styles; blind coders recorded whether bystanders helped, how fast, and for how long.

Result. Attractiveness didn't predict whether someone helped (p = .423), but an attractive-and-approachable style predicted significantly longer help — driven mostly by female helpers, not male ones as hypothesized.

  • Inter-rater Reliability
  • Chi-Square / ANOVA
